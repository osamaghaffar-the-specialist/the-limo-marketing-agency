Quick answer: how much does taxi dispatch software cost in the UK?

There is no single UK market price. Current public examples use flat monthly, per-driver weekly, per-vehicle monthly and package-based pricing. A headline subscription is only one part of total cost: branded apps, telephony, SMS, payment processing, APIs, data migration, support and contract terms can materially change the bill. This guide only publishes figures we could trace to vendor or authorised-reseller pages and labels quote-only pricing instead of guessing.

Important comparison rule

Weekly and monthly prices are intentionally left in the unit the supplier publishes. Converting a weekly figure to “monthly” with a simple four-week multiplier can understate an annual cost. For a like-for-like comparison, calculate weekly plans over 52 weeks and monthly plans over 12 months, then add confirmed extras separately.

Why Two “Cheap” Dispatch Systems Can Cost Very Different Amounts

A published base fee tells you the billing unit, not necessarily the complete operating cost. One vendor may include dispatch, driver apps and web booking in the entry tier while another separates passenger apps, telephony, branding or integrations.

Driver vs vehicle pricing

Per-driver pricing grows with active driver count; per-vehicle pricing grows with activated vehicles. The cheaper model depends on how your fleet is staffed.

Package scope

A dispatch-only package should not be compared as equivalent to a package that also includes phone systems, customer apps, web booking and branding.

Fixed vs variable fees

Flat unlimited-driver plans become more attractive at a different fleet threshold than linear per-driver plans. Calculate your own break-even point.

Contract and switching cost

Setup, migration, minimum terms, cancellation periods and data export rules can matter as much as the monthly subscription.

The Hidden-Cost Checklist Before You Sign

Ask every supplier to complete the same checklist. If a cost is optional, record it as optional; if it is required for your operation, include it in your first-year total.

Setup & onboarding: implementation, tariff build, configuration and training.
Data migration: customers, accounts, drivers, bookings and historical records.
Driver licences: included quantity, active-driver definition and overage rate.
Passenger & driver apps: generic vs branded apps and store/development fees.
White-label branding: logos, colours, custom domains and app branding.
SMS & telephony: message rates, call channels, IVR, softphones and number ports.
Payments: gateway fees, card terminals, per-transaction charges and payout terms.
API & integrations: website booking, accounting, flight data, marketplaces and custom API work.
Support & SLA: support hours, response times, dedicated account management and premium support.
Contract & exit: minimum term, notice period, renewal uplift, export access and cancellation costs.
Commission: whether the platform takes a percentage or fee from completed fares.
VAT: confirm whether displayed prices include or exclude VAT before comparing totals.

Do You Need New Dispatch Software — or Just a Better Booking Layer?

Some taxi, chauffeur and private-hire operators already have a capable dispatch platform such as Autocab, iCabbi, Cordic or TaxiCaller. Their bigger commercial problem is not dispatch; it is getting more high-intent traffic, converting that traffic into bookings and measuring which source produced the customer.

TLMA does not need to replace your dispatch system to improve the growth stack.

Where the existing platform is suitable, the growth layer can sit around it: SEO or Google Ads → conversion-focused website → quote/booking workflow → tracking → handoff into the operational stack.

  • Keep the software that already runs drivers and jobs.
  • Fix weak landing pages or booking friction.
  • Connect marketing sources to meaningful booking actions.
  • Only consider a dispatch migration when the operational case justifies the switching cost.

How to Compare Three Vendor Quotes Properly

Ask each supplier for the same information in the same order. A quote becomes useful only when the scope is comparable.

01
Define fleet inputs

Active vehicles, active drivers, controllers, locations, monthly jobs and expected growth.

02
Define required channels

Phone, web booking, passenger app, corporate booking, hotel/reception booking and third-party marketplaces.

03
Request mandatory and optional fees separately

Licence, setup, migration, app, SMS/phone, payment, API/integration, support and hardware.

04
Calculate 12-month and first-year cost

Annualise weekly prices over 52 weeks and monthly prices over 12 months. Add one-off setup/migration fees only to first-year cost.

05
Record commercial terms

VAT, minimum term, cancellation notice, renewal rules, service level, data export and switching obligations.
